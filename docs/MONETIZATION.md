# Cost-Recovery Options

This is a decision document, not a price list or an offer. The repository does not currently establish an active paid tier, a request quota, a certification programme, grant eligibility, sponsor income, or cost coverage.

## 1. Product and trust constraints

Any future revenue model must preserve the evidence boundary:

- payment must never buy a `VERIFIED` label;
- free and paid retrieval must expose the same evidence status and limitations for the same record;
- commercial copy must not turn fixture results or database row counts into broad performance claims;
- personal data, query text, source code, and telemetry must not be sold or used for advertising;
- private customer data must not enter the public catalog without a separate explicit agreement and technical segregation;
- a paid service must not be announced until pricing, taxes, billing, cancellation, support scope, availability, and privacy terms have been implemented and reviewed.

## 2. Plausible future models

### Voluntary sponsorship

Repository sponsorship could support hosting while keeping the public evidence registry accessible. Before adding a donation or sponsorship link, document the recipient, payment provider, privacy implications, tax handling, and whether the payment is a donation, sponsorship, or purchase.

### Metered operational service

A future hosted tier could charge for measurable operational features such as higher rate limits, dedicated capacity, private deployment support, or longer customer-controlled retention. The core evidence status must remain identical across tiers.

No request limits or prices are specified here because capacity and demand have not been measured. Publish them only after load tests, cost modelling, and billing implementation exist.

### Private deployments and support

Organisations may value deployment assistance, maintenance, or a segregated private registry. This requires explicit data-controller/processor roles, access controls, backup and deletion procedures, incident handling, support boundaries, and contractual review. The current public service is not a private enterprise mesh.

### Independent verification services

An operator could charge for running a separately governed review or for producing signed evidence artifacts. The result must still be pass, fail, or unverified according to the same contract. A badge must identify its exact scope, toolchain, date, issuer, and limitations; it must not imply security certification or regulatory approval.

### Grants

Open-source infrastructure programmes may be worth investigating. Eligibility rules, funding windows, amounts, intellectual-property conditions, reporting duties, and applicant status change over time and must be checked against the programme's current primary documentation before an application. The project must not describe itself as eligible or funded until that is confirmed.

## 3. Evidence required before launch

Before activating any cost-recovery model, collect and review:

1. measured hosting and maintenance costs;
2. observed demand and capacity under realistic load;
3. an implemented billing and cancellation flow;
4. tax, invoicing, consumer-information, and privacy requirements for the operator's jurisdiction and customer type;
5. a security and data-flow review for every payment or customer-data provider;
6. terms that distinguish compatibility evidence from warranty, certification, or professional advice;
7. a process for refunds, disputes, service changes, and end-of-service data export or deletion.

Legal and tax review should be obtained before accepting payment. Repository documentation is not a substitute for professional advice.

## 4. Decision rule

Do not monetise the `VERIFIED` label itself. If future revenue is needed, charge for scarce operational resources or human services, publish only measured capabilities, and keep the evidence semantics independent of payment.
